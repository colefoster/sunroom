**The corrected evidence does not change my scores or ranking.** It provides clearer support for A’s instructional strengths and confirms the mobile layout tradeoff previously visible in its full-page image.

- **B has the more coherent visual composition.** Its rounded headings, restrained peach accents, green-black surfaces and spacious arrangement work consistently across desktop and mobile. The strongly outlined tint rectangle clearly separates the movable overlay from the shared window. A’s bold typography has character, but its dense scene, status information and control panel create more competing detail.
- **A makes the controls and installation easier to understand.** The new mobile control image clearly shows paired “Locked / Clicks pass through” and “Unlocked / Move & resize” choices. These explain state better than B’s switch plus supporting text. The corrected installation image reinforces A’s advantage: an explicit source-build heading, numbered steps and plainly stated absence of a signed download.
- **B keeps controls closer to the mobile demonstration.** Its supplementary image places the panel immediately beneath the scene. A’s corrected mobile demo shows a terminal below the shared window, followed by two rows of opacity samples before the main settings panel. The samples are useful, but this arrangement increases scrolling between the shaded area and its settings.

## One material weakness each

- **A:** Mobile settings are too far from the shaded window for convenient adjustment and visual comparison.
- **B:** Small supporting text weakens discoverability, particularly the hero’s drag/lock instructions. The excellent headline treatment does not extend to equally readable interaction guidance.

## Evidence and limitations

All five corrected/supplemental images were inspected directly. Visual conclusions come from screenshots; working dragging, keyboard movement/resizing, click-through, independent visibility/lock states, persistence and overflow checks remain supported by the supplied QA receipts, not newly performed tests.

Some corrected desktop imagery is soft; I did not treat capture softness as a design defect. No physical-touch, assistive-technology, native-installation or OS-level behavior was verified. The ranking remains blind and final.
